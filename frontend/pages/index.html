    Where the money leaks

    A missed call used to cost you nothing. Now it costs you the rental.

    He used to call back in five minutes. Now the national answers at midnight, so he just calls them instead. Nothing about your yard got worse. The cost of missing him did.

    01

    The missed call

    Lunch rush, after hours, everyone loading a truck, and the phone rings out. A renter doesn't leave voicemail twice. He calls the next yard on the map.

    You never find out he called.

    02

    The dead quote

    You priced the job, sent it over, and nothing. Nobody has time to chase quotes, so "let me check with my guy" quietly becomes somebody else's contract.

    The pile only gets older.

    03

    The quiet account

    Contractors who rented from you for ten years, then stopped. No complaint, no goodbye. They just started renting from whoever picked up that Tuesday.

    They still have credit on file with you.

    No rental platform on the market tracks how long your yard takes to answer a quote, or how many of those quotes you win. You cannot feel a leak you never metered.

    How it answers

    Two tracks. The equipment decides which, not the customer.

    A contractor describes the job. The machine settles what happens next.

    Instant Reserve

    For machines a number can describe.

    Dig depth, working height, transport width. If the spec sheet can answer it, the system matches the job, checks what is actually free on those dates, and holds the unit. At ten at night, without waiting on anybody.

    Quote Required

    For anything off a load chart.

    Crane capacity changes with radius, so it is never priced automatically. Not by us, not by software, not ever. The job lands with your estimator as a complete brief. He still prices it. He just stops ringing people back to ask what the job is.

    Every owner in this business has been pitched software that claims it can quote a lift. We refuse to. That is the point.

    What does it cost?+

    We don't put the number on a web page, and there's a reason that isn't evasive. The price only means anything next to what your leak is actually worth, and we don't know that until we've called your yard and you've answered six questions. So: run the scan, take the twenty minutes, and you'll get the number from a person alongside your own figures. If your leak turns out to be small, we'll tell you that and there'll be nothing to sell.

    What we can tell you now is the anchor. Owners usually fix this by hiring a rental salesperson. Sixty to ninety thousand base, plus commission, payroll tax and a truck. Ninety days to hire, six months to find out whether he's any good. We cost less than that, we start in a week, and we come with three guarantees attached.

    Why only five yards at a time?+

    Because our people are on your phones, using your name. That is not something you can do for forty yards at once and still do well. Five is what we can carry properly through a ninety-day cycle, so five is what we take. When the fifth seat goes, we're full and we say so. We don't quietly take a sixth.

    What if the cohort is full when I'm ready?+

    Then you don't wait ninety days doing nothing. Deploy HeyDozr on your site now: the full rent-it-out system, quoting, live availability, booking. And your seat in the next cohort is booked the day it opens. You start catching inquiries this week instead of next quarter, and when the closers arrive your fleet is already built, so the ninety days starts at full speed rather than in setup.

    It's the better path for most yards anyway. You get the machine working before you add people to it.

    I don't want strangers calling my customers.+

    Good. If you were relaxed about that we'd be worried about you.

    They won't be strangers and they won't sound like a call centre. They answer as your yard, with your name, from a number that's yours, working from scripts you approve before a single call goes out. They quote only inside pricing rules you set, and anything outside those rules goes straight to your estimator with a complete brief. For the first two weeks you review every call recording before we widen it out.

    We already have rental software.+

    You should, and we don't replace it. Nothing gets ripped out, nothing gets migrated, and nobody on your team learns a new system.

    Your rental software is very good at what happens after the contract is signed: on rent, off rent, returned, rent-ready, billing. The leak is upstream of all that. It's the gap between an inquiry arriving and somebody selling it, and no rental platform has ever picked up a phone at 4:30 on a Friday.

    My counter already does follow-up.+

    Then the scan will show that, and we'll tell you so. The question isn't whether your people are good. It's whether every quote gets chased to a yes or a no when the counter is buried, and what last month's un-chased ones were worth. Nobody has ever measured that at your yard, which is the entire point.

    Do you do this outside the US?+

    Yes. Independent yards in the US, Australia, and the Gulf: UAE, Oman, Qatar, Bahrain and Kuwait. Type your company name and the scan finds you the same way your customers do.

    Estimates come out in US dollars for now, with the arithmetic shown line by line, so restating any number in your own rates takes a minute and a calculator.

    What happens to my customer list?+

    It stays yours. We use it only for your work, never share, sell or reuse it, and delete it on request when we're done. That's in writing, in the agreement. You also don't hand over a database on day one. We start with thirty to fifty recent inquiries and your open quote pile, in a workspace of your own.

    What happens after the ninety days?+

    Nothing you paid for disappears. You either keep us managed and we keep working the pipeline, or the closers come off and you keep the machine: the quoting system on your site plus everything behind it, including the response reporting you'll have been reading every Monday.

    Either way you keep the fleet build, the rate cards and the scripts. That's the whole design: ninety days proves the number, and the system is what makes it repeatable once we're gone.
