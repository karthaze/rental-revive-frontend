What's leaking

Nothing about your yard got worse. The cost of missing him did.

He used to call back in five minutes. Now the national answers at midnight, so he just calls them instead. Your fleet is the same, your rates are the same, your people are the same. What changed is what happens in the gap between a contractor wanting your machine and somebody at your yard having a minute to get back to him.

Leak 01

The missed call.

Lunch rush, after hours, everyone out loading a truck, and the phone rings out.

A renter does not leave voicemail twice. He works down the map, and the yard that picks up gets the job — not the yard with the better machine or the better rate.

The part that makes this the worst of the four is that it leaves no trace. A quote you lost is at least a quote you know about. A call that rang out at 5:40 on a Friday is invisible on Monday. Nobody at your counter did anything wrong, nobody reports it, and it never appears in any number you look at.

You never find out he called.

Leak 02

The slow quote.

Not the lowest number. The first one.

A contractor with a machine down at seven in the morning is not running a procurement exercise. He wants to know he has a machine. The yard that tells him yes first usually keeps him, and he stops calling after that.

Every hour a quote sits unanswered is an hour somebody else is answering. On the segments we scan, quotes that go out same-day close at a completely different rate from quotes that go out next-day — and almost nobody in this industry measures the difference, because no system tells them.

Leak 03

The dead quote pile.

You priced the job, sent it over, and nothing came back.

"Let me check with my guy" quietly becomes somebody else's contract. Not because your price was wrong, but because nobody has an hour spare to chase it and the pile only gets older.

This is the cheapest money in the building and the one every yard skips. The work is already qualified — a real customer described a real job and you already spent the time pricing it. All that is missing is somebody working it to a yes, a no, or a reason.

A quoted job that went quiet is not a dead lead. It is a job someone else is doing next month.

Leak 04

The quiet account.

Contractors who rented from you for ten years, then stopped.

No complaint, no goodbye, no cancelled account. They just started renting from whoever picked up that Tuesday, and by the time anyone notices it has been two quarters.

They still have credit on file with you. They know your yard, your people and your gate. Reactivation is a conversation, not a mailshot — which is exactly why it never happens on its own.

Why none of this shows up

You cannot feel a leak you never metered.

Ask your rental software what last month's average response time was. It does not know. Not because it is bad software — because nobody ever asked it to.

Rental platforms are built for what happens after the contract is signed: on rent, off rent, returned, rent-ready, billing, maintenance. That part is usually well run at the yards we scan.

All four leaks sit upstream of that. They happen between an inquiry arriving and somebody selling it, which is the one stretch of the process no system on the market watches. Not one of them tracks how long your yard takes to answer a quote request, or what share of those quotes you win.

So the leak does not show up as a bad number. It shows up as no number at all.
