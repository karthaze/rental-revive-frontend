What we write down

Three timestamps and what happened.

Answered on the third ring. Rang out after forty seconds. Straight to voicemail. That is the whole record, and it is the only number in the entire scan that is not either public information or something you typed in yourself.

If all three are answered fast, the scan says so and tells you your counter is holding. That result happens, we do not hide it, and there is nothing to sell you afterwards.

The arithmetic

Every assumption is conservative on purpose.

A scan that flatters the number is worthless to you, because you would find out on day thirty. So each input is set below what the published industry figures would justify, and each one is shown.

What we assume, and why it is low
Calls we catch that actually rent3 in 10
Quotes chased that close25–50% by segment
Inquiry to booked, compounded20–40%
Published industry benchmark38–48%
Where our numbers sitAt or below it

The close rates come out of published rental-industry data, then we cut them. Slow project-quote segments like cranes and specialty sit furthest under the average. Counter-speed segments like compact and aerial sit at the top-quartile line their walk-in mix justifies.

Ticket bands are checked against published 2026 rate data per segment rather than invented: an excavator at roughly $719 a day and $2,021 a week, a scissor lift at $495 a week, an operated mobile crane at $1,200 to $2,500 a day before operator, transport and rigging.

You will still be able to argue with a number. Good. Change the input and the total moves in front of you, which is the point of showing the working.

What the scan will never do: promise you a ranking, promise a citation in an AI assistant, or put a revenue figure on a page it cannot show you the multiplication for.
